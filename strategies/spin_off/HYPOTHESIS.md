# Spin-off: the new company in its first weeks, after the parent's holders sell it

Written 2026-11-17 23:34 ET, before anything is measured. When a company spins off a part, its
shareholders receive shares they did not choose: index funds that hold the parent but not the
new company's index, and managers whose mandate it does not fit, sell in the first days and
weeks. Published work (from the 1990s) finds the new company outperforms over the following
year; whether that survives, and whether it is better bought straight away or after the first
two weeks of selling, is the question.

## Events

Sharadar corporate actions, action "spinoff", dated at the spin, read before 2023-11-09, from
2005 to 13 October 2023. The new company (contraticker) must appear in the price table for the
first time within a week of that date (a new listing), be common stock, close at $3 or more on
its first day and trade $1M or more that day.

## Trades (3 cells, family `spin_off`)

1. **New company, early:** bought at the open of its second session, held 60 sessions.
2. **New company, late:** bought at the open of its eleventh session, held 60 sessions.
3. **Parent:** bought at the open of the session after the spin date, held 60 sessions
   (common stock, $300M or more).

Costs: the auction allowance by the stock's dollar volume (the first day's for cell 1, the
20-day average for cells 2 and 3) and commission. Each trade is measured against the average
common stock of $300M or more with $1M a day, bought at the same open and held as long.

## What counts

Search 2010–2017: 2% a trade over that average, weekly t of 3, six years of eight, positive
after costs; there are about 25 spin-offs a year, so the count is reported and does not stop a
cell. Tests on 2005–2009 and 2018–2022 at 1%, t of 2.5, four years of five. 2023 is a check.
The holdout stays sealed.
