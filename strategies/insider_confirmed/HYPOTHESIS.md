# Insider buying, confirmed: insiders bought, then the market agreed

Written 2026-11-17 23:02 ET, before anything is measured. The two rules that have passed here are rule I
(buy when insiders of a small company report buying $100,000 or more) and A50 (buy a small
company the session after results lift it 15% on five times its volume). Rule I acts on the
informed party alone and its edge has halved since 2018; A50 waits for the market to confirm.
This puts them together: an insider purchase, then, within a few weeks, a strong rise on heavy
volume, whatever its cause. The insiders knew; the market has started to agree, and in small
companies belief is slow.

## Events

Rule I's events (its research signals: $50M to $300M, $100,000 or more bought on one filing
date, one a company in 21 days), 2005 to 13 October 2023. Then the **first session** among the
next 10 (or 20) after the signal session where the two-session return is at least 10% on three
times normal two-session dollar volume (or 15% on five times, A50's thresholds), in a company
still of $50M to $300M. Bought at the next open, sold at the close 15 sessions after that
session. Costs as A50.

## Cells

2 confirmations × 2 windows = **4**, family `insider_confirmed`. Shown beside each: how many
are also A50 signals.

## What counts

As A50's search: 2010–2017, 1% a trade after costs, weekly t of 3, six years of eight, 100
trades (fewer reported and taken on if the rest is met). Tests on 2006–2009 (where rule I's
insider data starts) and 2018–2022, each cell that goes forward run once: 0.5%, t of 2.5,
three years of four and four of five. 2023 is a check. The holdout stays sealed.

**Results, 2026-11-17 23:03 ET.** In REPORT.md. **None of the 4 cells meets the bar.** In 2010–2017 the
confirmed purchases made 1.25% to 3.51% a trade after costs, with **negative medians** (−1.2%
to −2.1%) and a weekly t of 0.8 to 1.6. Waiting for the market to agree makes rule I worse,
not better (rule I itself made 3.2% a trade in these years). 4 trials. No tests run.
