# Annual timing: a company that files its annual report much earlier than last year

Written 2026-11-18 00:37 ET, before anything is measured. Managers delay bad news and hurry good news;
published work finds that companies filing their annual reports earlier than usual tend to
have better news and better returns afterwards. The results release (8-K) usually comes
first, but the annual report also carries the audit, the risk factors and the full accounts;
an annual report filed well ahead of the company's own habit is a sign the audit went cleanly.

## Events

SEC filing times (the owner's download), form 10-K, accepted before 2023-11-09, 2005 to 13
October 2023, by a filer Sharadar maps to a stock. **Early:** filed 10 or more days earlier in
the calendar year than the same company's previous 10-K (and that one 300 to 430 days before).
Bought at the first open after acceptance. Common stock, $2 or more, $250,000 a day or more, in
two sizes ($50M–$300M, $300M–$2B).

## Trade

Held 20 or 60 sessions. Costs: the auction allowance and commission. Measured against the
average common stock of the same size bought at the same open and held as long.

## Cells

size × hold = **4**, family `annual_timing`.

## What counts

Search 2010–2017: 150 trades, 1% over the average for 20 sessions or 2% for 60, weekly t of
3, six years of eight, positive after costs. Tests on 2005–2009 and 2018–2022 at half, t 2.5,
four of five. 2023 is a check. The holdout stays sealed.
