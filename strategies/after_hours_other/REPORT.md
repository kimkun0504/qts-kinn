**No cell meets the bar.** Outside results, a large move after hours does not carry on
overnight in either direction. Two things stand out:

1. **A rise after hours with no 8-K gives back.** Bought at the minute's high, these lost 1.4%
   to 4.0% a trade, and in every one of the eight years to the next close. Looking at a
   sample: some are results whose 8-K came later or that file 6-Ks (foreign companies), and a
   few are single odd prints that were gone a minute later; the rest are rises on no filing
   at all. The opposite trade (selling these short) is a new idea, planned below before it is
   measured.
2. **This reading weakens the after_hours lead.** Read at the first minute 10% away instead of
   half an hour after the filing, and at 10% instead of 15%, the results short makes 0.65% a
   trade (t 0.74) rather than 2.8%. The lead's edge sits in the larger falls and the later
   reading; it is narrower than "after hours is slow" would suggest.
