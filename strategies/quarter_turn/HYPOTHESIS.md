# Quarter turn: the half-year's worst losers bought at the last close of a quarter

Written 2026-11-17 23:57 ET, before anything is measured. Fund managers sell their losers before the end of
a quarter so they are not shown in the report (window dressing), and at the end of December
taxable holders sell them to book the loss. Both sales stop on the last day; the stocks they
pushed down can recover in the first days of the new quarter. Published work finds this most
in small losers at the turn of the year.

## Events

The last session of each calendar quarter, 2005 to the third quarter of 2023. Common stock,
$2 or more, $250,000 a day or more, in two sizes ($50M–$300M, $300M–$2B) by market value at
the previous close. **Losers:** the worst tenth of each size by return over the 120 sessions
to the previous close (known before a market-on-close order is sent).

## Trade

Bought at the close of the quarter's last session (a market-on-close order), sold at the
close of the tenth session of the new quarter. Costs: the auction allowance both ways and
commission. Measured against the average stock of the same size over the same window.

## Cells

turn (end of December, other quarter ends) × size = **4**, family `quarter_turn`.

## What counts

Search 2010–2017: 150 trades, 1% a trade over the average, weekly t of 3 (one week per event,
so this is a t over the quarters), six years of eight, positive after costs. Tests on
2005–2009 and 2018–2022 at 0.5%, t of 2.5, four years of five. 2023 is a check. The holdout
stays sealed.
