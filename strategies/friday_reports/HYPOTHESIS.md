# Friday reports: the drift after results that were read on a Friday

Written 2026-11-17 22:43 ET, before anything is measured. A published result (DellaVigna and Pollet,
2009): when results come out on a Friday, the first reaction is smaller and more of the move
comes later, because fewer people are paying attention. The one rule that works here (A50)
trades a slow belief in small companies. In companies of $300M to $2B that drift has been
too weak to trade since 2018 (family 22). If inattention adds to it, it may be tradable in the
Friday reports alone, and stronger in small companies too.

## Events

Every results 8-K with its **reaction session R** (the first session whose close comes after
the SEC accepted it; the table built for family 24). A **Friday report** is one whose R is a
Friday or the last session before a market holiday. The signal is read on the session after R
(R+1, a Monday as a rule), as A50 reads its own: the return over R and R+1, and their dollar
volume against normal. Bought at the next open, sold at the close 15 sessions after the signal.
Costs as A50. 2005 to 13 October 2023.

## Cells

A rise of 15% or more, or of 10% or more, on five times normal volume; companies of $50M to
$300M, or $300M to $2B. **4 cells**, family `friday_reports`. Each is shown beside the same
rule on all other days (not a cell; the comparison).

## What counts

Search 2010–2017: 1% a trade after costs, weekly t of 3, six years of eight, 100 trades (fewer
reported and taken on if the rest is met). Tests on 2005–2009 and 2018–2022, each cell that
goes forward run once: 0.5%, t of 2.5, four years of five, in both. 2023 is a check. The holdout
stays sealed.
