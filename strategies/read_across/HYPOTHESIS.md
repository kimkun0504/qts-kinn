# Read-across: a confirmed jump on results, and the peers that have not reported yet

Written 2026-11-17 21:03 ET, before anything is measured. The owner asked for earlier signals. Tonight's
`before_report` showed that a company's own past reports do not predict its next one. This
asks the other question: does **another company's** report, this season, in the same line of
business?

## Why

The earnings rule works because a confirmed jump in a small company is believed slowly. If a
strong quarter is partly about the industry (prices, demand), the same slow belief should
apply to the companies in that industry that report later, and they can be bought **before**
their own report. Industry momentum as such was tried in the search of 16 November (family
15) and found nothing; this uses the confirmed jump as the trigger and only the peers still
to report.

## Signal

On day S a **trigger** is any stock that meets the earnings rule's signal without its size
cap: results, a two-session rise of 15% or more on five times normal dollar volume, market
value $50M or more. A **peer** on day S is a common stock in the same industry (the vendor's
150-odd industries) with a market value of $50M or more, whose own last results were 40 to
75 sessions ago (so its report is still to come), and which is not itself a trigger or
reporting that day. A peer is taken once: not again within 20 days of an earlier signal.

**Buy at the next open; sell at the close 5, 10 or 15 sessions after S.** Costs as the
earnings rule (auction allowance and commission).

## Cells

Peer size ($50M to $300M; $300M to $2B; $2B or more) × 3 holds × 2 states of the peer
(every peer; only peers that have not moved, a two-session return under 3%) = **18**, family
`read_across`.

## What counts

Search 2010–2017: 150 trades, 1% after costs, weekly t of 3, six years of eight. Tests on
2005–2009 and 2018–2022, each cell that goes forward run once: 0.5%, t of 2.5, four years of
five, in both. Each cell is also shown over the average stock for the same days, since a
long trade of 5 to 15 sessions carries the market. The holdout stays sealed.
