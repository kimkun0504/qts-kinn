# Uplisting: a company that moves up to Nasdaq or the NYSE

Written 2026-11-17 23:27 ET, before anything is measured. What pays here is slow belief in small companies
that few people watch. A company that moves from the over-the-counter market or NYSE American
to Nasdaq or the NYSE becomes visible to, and buyable by, many more funds at once (index
eligibility, mandates that forbid other markets, margin). If that money comes over weeks, the
price should drift up after the move.

## Events

Sharadar's corporate actions (exchange changes, read before 2023-11-09): a move **from OTC or
NYSE American to Nasdaq or the NYSE**, 2005 to 13 October 2023. **D** is the first session on or
after the move with a price in the panel. Listed common stock, market value $50M or more, dollar
volume of $250,000 or more on D, a close of $1 or more.

## Trade

Bought at the open of D+1 (after the first session on the new market), sold at the close 20 or
60 sessions later. Costs: the auction allowance (by D's dollar volume) and commission. Each trade
is measured against every stock of its size group bought at the same open and held as long.

Sizes: $50M to $300M; $300M to $2B.

## Cells

2 sizes × 2 holds = **4**, family `uplisting`.

## What counts

As `dividend_signal`: search 2010–2017 against the average stock, 1% a trade for 20 sessions
and 2% for 60, a weekly t of 3, six years of eight, positive after costs; 100 trades (fewer
reported and taken on if the rest is met). Tests on 2005–2009 and 2018–2022 at half those, t of
2.5, four years of five. 2023 is a check. The holdout stays sealed.

**Results, 2026-11-17 23:28 ET.** **None of the 4 cells meets the bar.** 2010–2017, against the average
stock: $50M–$300M −2.06% (20 sessions) and −5.42% (60), one or two years of eight positive;
$300M–$2B +0.59% and −0.97%. Small companies that move up trail other stocks. 4 trials.
