# Before the report: buying ahead of results, chosen by the company's own record

Written 2026-11-17 20:57 ET, before anything is measured. The owner asked for ways "to enter earlier to
generate earlier signals". The one rule that works here buys **after** a strong report has
been confirmed. The earliest possible entry is before the report itself.

## Why

Results are not independent from one quarter to the next: a company whose business has
turned tends to beat again. If the market only half believes it, the stock that jumped on
its last report (and held the jump) should do better than others through its next one. The
indicator is the company's own record of reactions, which no price chart carries.

The search of 16 November tried "results due within 15 sessions" as a calendar condition
(family 12f) and found nothing. It did not use the record of past reactions, and it bought
at the next open for a fixed 5 to 15 sessions rather than around the report.

## Events and indicator

Every results 8-K, 2005-01-03 to 2023-10-13, with its **reaction session R** (the first
session whose close comes after the SEC accepted it; the table of family 24). Reports of the
same company less than 30 sessions apart count once. Common stock; market value $50M or
more and $250,000 a day five sessions before R.

A past report's **reaction** is the close of the session after R against the close before R
(two sessions, as the earnings rule reads it). Groups, all known before entry:

- **Repeater:** the last report (40 to 90 sessions earlier) had a reaction of +10% or more.
- **Streak:** the last two reports both had a reaction of +5% or more.
- **All:** every report (the base, and a trade in itself if it meets the bar).

## Trades

**Buy at the close five sessions before R.** Sell at the close of the session before R
(the run-up alone, no report held), or at the close of the session after R (through the
report, to the point where the earnings rule would be buying). Costs: the auction allowance
and commission, both ways.

Sizes: $50M to $300M, $300M to $2B, $2B or more. Cells: 3 groups × 3 sizes × 2 exits =
**18**, family `before_report`.

## What counts

Search 2010–2017: 150 trades, 1% after costs, weekly t of 3, six years of eight. Tests on
2005–2009 and 2018–2022, each cell that goes forward run once: 0.5%, t of 2.5, four years of
five, in both. 2023 is a check for a cell that passes. The holdout stays sealed. The report
gives the worst trade and worst 1% of any cell that holds a report.

## The assumption

The trade needs the report date five sessions ahead. Most companies announce it one to three
weeks ahead; some small ones give little notice. The test uses the actual date, so a cell
that passes is then re-run with the date **predicted** from the company's own calendar a
year earlier, and has to survive that before it is believed.

**Results, 2026-11-17 20:58 ET.** In REPORT.md. **None of the 18 cells meets the bar; the best makes
0.29% a trade.** A company's past reactions say nothing about its next one: after a report
that jumped 10% or more, the next report's two-session reaction averaged about zero, the
same as every report. The five sessions before a report gained nothing either (−0.5% after
costs under $300M, about zero above). Holding small companies through the report lost 0.9%
a trade in the search years. 18 trials. No tests run.
